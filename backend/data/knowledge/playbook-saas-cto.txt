Sales Playbook — US SaaS CTO / VP Engineering Outreach

ICP definition: CTOs and VPs of Engineering at US SaaS companies with 50-500 employees, running
AWS, GCP or Azure with Kubernetes, and showing a cloud-cost signal: a FinOps or platform hire, a
recent funding round, or public commentary on cloud spend. Exclude competitors and companies
under 50 employees, where cloud spend is rarely a line-item worth optimising.

Positioning: Lead with the outcome (cloud waste found in days, without a migration or an
engineering quarter), not the product. The buyer's fear is losing release velocity, so stress
read-only access and that findings surface inside the dashboards the team already uses.

Best proof point: Fleetwise cut cloud spend 23% in 60 days on a multi-region AWS + Kubernetes +
Grafana footprint. Cite it by name only for comparable stacks.

Example first email (hiring signal):
Subject: Cloud spend at {company}
Hi {first_name} - saw {company} is hiring a Cloud FinOps Engineer, which usually means spend has
outgrown the tooling. We helped a ~240-person logistics SaaS on a similar AWS + Kubernetes setup
cut cloud spend 23% in 60 days with no migration, via read-only access. Worth a 15-minute look?

Example first email (funding signal):
Subject: After the raise, before the AWS bill
Hi {first_name} - congrats on the round. Post-raise is when cloud costs start compounding faster
than revenue. NimbusGuard flags waste in compute, storage and data transfer without touching your
release process. Open to a short call to see what it would find on your footprint?

Example follow-up (no reply after 3 days): Keep it to two lines, add one new fact (a different
capability or the case study), and never repeat the first message. Stop after the campaign's
touch limit.

Rules: one specific, verifiable fact about the prospect per message. Never invent a metric.
Keep first emails under 90 words. Book a meeting; do not pitch pricing in the first touch.
